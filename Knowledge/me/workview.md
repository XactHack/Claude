# WorkView

Work is how I translate who I am into something the world can feel.

It's not just a function — it's the arena where my values get tested, my skills get sharpened, and my purpose gets expressed. For me, work is meaningful when it sits at the intersection of challenge, impact, and growth. If those three aren't present, I'm not working — I'm just busy.

I work because I'm wired to build things. Not just systems or platforms, but capability in people, order out of complexity, and confidence where there was once confusion. The TeamLab onboarding, the ServiceNow transformation, the multi-park infrastructure overhaul — these weren't just delivery wins. They were proof that a clear vision, the right team, and relentless execution can turn chaos into something extraordinary.

Good work looks like this: a problem worth solving, a team worth leading, and a standard worth holding. It demands precision — the kind you develop when you know that a miscalculation at 12,000 feet isn't theoretical. It rewards courage — the willingness to #GoFirst when the path isn't clear and the stakes are real. And it requires you to leave people better than you found them, not just projects.

Work is also where I grow fastest. Every complex program is a dojo. Every stakeholder challenge is a sparring partner. I don't separate the professional from the personal — the same discipline that earned me a black belt built my PMP, and the same resilience that got me back in the tunnel after injury got me back into IT after a decade away.

The measure of good work isn't just delivery. It's the question I ask myself at the end: **Did I add value, or just add hours?**
